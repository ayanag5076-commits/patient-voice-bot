# Architecture

The bot is a LiveKit Agents worker that plays a patient calling a clinic. `run.py` picks a scenario and dispatches the agent. The agent joins a LiveKit room and dials `TARGET_NUMBER` (refusing anything not in `ALLOWED_NUMBERS`) through LiveKit SIP over a Twilio Elastic SIP trunk. Twilio is the SIP carrier LiveKit documents, and it records the call in dual channel, with the bot on one track and the clinic on the other. That captures exactly what went over the phone line, which is more trustworthy than recording inside the app. The voice side is a pipeline rather than a speech-to-speech model, so there's text at every step, which makes debugging much easier. Deepgram Nova-3 handles speech to text because it's fast and accurate on phone audio, names, and numbers. An OpenAI chat model plays the patient, with reasoning turned off so it replies quickly. Each scenario gives it a persona, a goal, and sometimes a behavior to test, like interrupting or rambling. Cartesia turns the reply into speech because it starts talking quickly. To keep latency low, every stage streams, prompts stay short, and reasoning is off. Silero VAD plus LiveKit's turn detector decide when the clinic has finished talking. The endpointing delay defaults to 1.2 seconds because receptionists often pause mid-sentence; the barge-in scenario drops it to 0.2 seconds on purpose to test interruptions.

A few guards came from real calls. OpenAI rejects tool calls when reasoning is on, so it's off. Bots tend to treat a recorded notice or language menu as the end of the call, so the prompt tells the patient to wait through them, and the hang-up tool only works after 45 seconds and a goodbye. When the tool refuses, the bot is told the call is still connected and no follow-up turn is generated, so it doesn't announce a hang-up that never happened. Running each scenario at least twice separates repeatable bugs from one-offs. After each batch, `transcribe_calls.py` splits the two recording channels and transcribes each side with Whisper, and `analyze.py` drafts bug candidates for a human to confirm by listening. Known limits: the bot sometimes repeats itself when the clinic is slow, and Whisper occasionally invents text over silence. Automatic per-scenario scoring would be a good next step.
